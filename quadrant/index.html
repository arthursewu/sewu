The four-box diagnostic

Is it a creative problem or a spend problem?

Both look the same from the dashboard. All you see is an ad that is not working.

So people guess. They fix the thing they are familiar with, and hope that was the problem. That is how one bad ad turns into a month of work and a hole in the account.

This is the check I run before I touch anything.

Two questions, not one

Everyone judges an ad on ROAS alone. That is the mistake. You need two answers before you know what you are looking at.

  • Did it earn spend? Did the algorithm choose to give this ad budget, against everything else in the campaign. This is share of spend, not the number you set.
  • Did it hit the ROAS bar? Measured against the campaign median, not against your hopes.

All four boxes turn on spend share, not ROAS. That is the part almost everyone gets backwards.

Five rules that bite

These cost me more than the framework saved me, until I wrote them down.

1. Labels migrate. Never call it at day 7.

A week-one KPI Winner becoming a week-two Breakthrough is the normal path, not the exception. In my own data a week-one Loser took 55% of spend in week two.

2. A Breakthrough is not a high-ROAS ad.

Breakthroughs settle 2 to 9% below benchmark by week three while holding 45 to 90% of the spend. Decay at scale is the cost of scale, not a kill signal. I have watched one sit at benchmark while taking 75% of its campaign.

3. A starved window is not evidence.

If the last stretch ran below about 3× your CPA per day, it cannot contain a conversion. A zero in that window is expected whether the ad works or not. Judge the last funded window, not the tail.

4. In ABO, spend share is not earned.

You set the budgets, so the spend axis reflects your decision and not the algorithm's verdict. In an ABO or target-CPA campaign, read the classification as ROAS only.

5. Read the median, never the pooled group.

One ad with strange traffic becomes the pooled benchmark and manufactures a deficit for everything else in the campaign.

Running it on your own account

Export from Ads Manager broken down by day. Without a daily grain there are no weeks and none of this computes.

You need spend and ROAS to place the ad in a box. To know which layer to fix, add the pre-click columns (impressions, reach, frequency, CPM, CTR, hook rate, hold rate) and the post-click ones (link clicks, landing page views, adds to cart, checkouts initiated).

One thing that will silently ruin the read: match ad sets by substring, then read the matched names back. Meta renames ad sets when you edit them, so one asset shows up under several names. Exact matching drops half of it and you will not notice. That mistake once turned a 16% deficit into a 4% surplus in my own numbers.
