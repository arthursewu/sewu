The Scale Ladder

Four stages. Most accounts are stuck on the first one.

You cannot fix a number that isn't real. Each stage has one exit condition — clear it and the next problem becomes the one worth solving.

0 BLIND 1 MISDIAGNOSED 2 ROUTED 3 SCALING
the number is lying real problem, wrong suspect one lever, named the constraint moves to volume
What your ROAS says Platform ROAS, taken at face value. Audits in this corpus read 36×, 13.6×, 12.84× at platform level. You know platform is inflated but still argue from one number. Three numbers, not one: platform → 7-day click → incremental, compared to acquisition MER. MER is the number that runs the business. Platform ROAS is a diagnostic, not a scoreboard.
Attribution View-through unexamined. Audited accounts sat at 60–70%; one at 90% reporting 14× ROAS. You strip 1-day view from campaigns — which breaks optimisation. Keep 1-day view for optimisation. Change reporting only. Bands: <20% fine · 20–25% watch · >30% switch to 7-day click. Reporting reconciled to the P&L. One account cutting to click-only: spend −13%, revenue +7%.
What you blame “Meta is broken.” Creative, media buying or funnel — before checking whether the problem is yours at all. Triage first: no problem · real but not yours · yours, self‑inflicted · unearned scale. The bucket is named before anyone opens Ads Manager.
Your first move Change the budget. Rewrite creative for a problem that happened before the click. Turn the high-spending “losers” back on. Recovers roughly 30% of spend at the same target, almost always. Nothing is turned off without a kill condition set before launch.
Creative Volume with no read — you cannot tell which ad is carrying the account. More ads, same idea. Diversity is assumed, not measured. Concept diversity is the constraint, not asset count. Enough distinct concepts that fatigue in one does not stall the account.
Offer & AOV Not in the conversation. Treated as a merchandising problem, not a bidding one. Bidding blind to order value is buying your cheapest customer. Order value is an input you control, not an outcome you report.
Bottom line Nothing you decide here is real. You fix the wrong thing and pay for it twice. One lever, and you know which. Spend goes up without efficiency falling off a cliff.

Every figure above comes from a 519-transcript corpus of working practitioners, cross-checked across three independent sources. Where those sources disagree, the disagreement is recorded rather than averaged away.
